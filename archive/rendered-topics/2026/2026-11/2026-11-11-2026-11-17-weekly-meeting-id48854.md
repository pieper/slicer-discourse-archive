## Post #3 by @ebrahim (2026-11-17 22:52 UTC)

<p>Notes from today’s meeting.</p>
<h3><a name="p-137017-discussion-on-large-prs-1" class="anchor" href="#p-137017-discussion-on-large-prs-1" aria-label="Heading link"></a>Discussion on large PRs</h3>
<p>Discussion started here: <a href="https://github.com/Slicer/Slicer/pull/9554" class="inline-onebox">ENH: Add unified vector field display for transforms, models and vector volumes using layer DM by lassoan · Pull Request #9554 · Slicer/Slicer · GitHub</a></p>
<p>And this is related to the larger AI policy discussion: <a href="https://github.com/Slicer/Slicer/issues/9529" class="inline-onebox">Come up with and document an AI policy · Issue #9529 · Slicer/Slicer · GitHub</a> and <a href="https://discourse.slicer.org/t/2026-11-10-weekly-meeting/48706" class="inline-onebox">2026.11.10 Weekly Meeting</a></p>
<p>(NOTE: the below is largely paraphrasing by Ebrahim, not direct quotes!)</p>
<p><strong>Andras</strong>: One option is to make the Slicer core small with everything more modular so that more of people’s large changes are addon-side rather than core-side and don’t impact each other. Would something like just MRML core be good? Should the core be almost… empty?</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1VdYQmLhHXFTl73D4SYF1R0iyfoxX0GK-y4YEYD5SmNw/edit?tab=t.0" class="inline-onebox">Slicer - process - scratchpad - 2026-11-16 - Google Docs</a></p>
<p>I like the idea of breaking apart Slicer core so that things are modular.</p>
<p>API and contracts between components need to be clean, and the core needs to be stable.</p>
<p>Strongly disagree with “discussion is not important” and “cost of significant changes in a PR is now low”.</p>
<p>Sure, a working prototype is now very fast.<br>
But creating a codebase that’s going to outlive our interest in maintaining it and scale well.</p>
<p>So I like the idea of having a fast-track (keep the experiments going) but then also having a dependable core.</p>
<p><strong>Andras</strong>: The performance of LLM-generated code gets better and better.<br>
Maybe just have to accept that things aren’t perfect and we will keep iterating and making it better and better.</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: A big reason why Slicer works so well is that it has such a large user base testing things.<br>
If you push too fast on the core, then people do not have time to test things and find issues.</p>
<p><strong>Andrey</strong>: Slicer is a research tool, though. It’s not supposed to be airtight and bug-free.</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: It’s not necessarily about airtightness, but at least there should be discussion when huge architectural changes are going into the core.<br>
Do they make sense for what people in the community are doing with Slicer?<br>
Does it break things for people?<br>
This discussion is what’s important, more important than the exact architecture being perfect.<br>
If we just let huge architectural changes into core…</p>
<p><strong>Andrey</strong>: I am not claiming to be qualified for the SW architecture perspective.<br>
But: humans also come with their own biases and issues.<br>
Claude, on the other hand, has no stake.</p>
<p>But I agree that we are becoming fragmented and want the community to come together, and I don’t know what the answer is.<br>
But back to this point about agents writing slop: do they really? Can we test this?</p>
<p><strong>Rafael</strong>: The world is confused right now, not just the Slicer community.<br>
Thibault, your concern is not primarily targeting the AI, but the AI is the elephant in the room enabling the problems being discussed.</p>
<p>As a community, we should take a stance and have some relationship with AI.<br>
Project Week will be a good venue to discuss this. I hope we can do that.</p>
<p>I think we should be especially cautious with the core.<br>
For example, the SOFA community generally does not accept AI-generated contributions to the <em>core</em>.</p>
<p><strong>Andras</strong>: Every 6 months (Project Week cycle), the situation is entirely different. Every 6 months, it’s not even worth comparing.</p>
<p><strong>Ebrahim</strong>: I think Thibault is currently one of the few who is actually a better SW architect than the frontier LLMs… but even that will stop being true.</p>
<p><strong>Rafael</strong>: We shouldn’t necessarily project into the future like that.</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: About the SW architecture thing… LLMs right now are good at what they’re trained on, but what they’re optimized to solve doesn’t always work for an actual project’s needs. They are optimized to address the prompts they’re given. They’re very good at sacrificing future for feature.</p>
<p><strong>Mauro</strong>: I find that in plan mode the LLM works really well, and it doesn’t repeat itself and create slop. How do you use it?</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: I use LLMs a lot, all day, every day. LLMs are extremely helpful, for example, to understand the overall design of something someone else wrote, e.g. to review large PRs the LLM can generate the docs that I actually want to look at to understand what’s going on and where. However, when it comes to overall SW architecture, I do that myself, because writing is thinking.</p>
<p><strong>Andras</strong>: We will need to find a process that doesn’t require us to agree. Because I don’t think I even agree with myself from 6 months ago.</p>
<p><strong>Rafael</strong>: We do not need to agree on how we use AI tools personally, but rather on the development process to follow. As AI-generated content increases, it’s going to become more and more difficult for humans to interface with it. (e.g. if we think everybody will use agents to write their emails, then everybody will need agents to read their email as well because of the amount of emails.)</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: We should have a way of handling version updates between MRML formats and establish a proper migration plan or versioning scheme.</p>
<p><strong>Andras</strong>: I have a branch that does this that I am testing.</p>
<p><strong>Thibault</strong>: About modularizing and breaking out the core:<br>
Scene migration. I don’t like that MRML nodes have legacy naming. There are also other design aspects that seem minor but really impact what people are able to do with the core.</p>
<p><strong>Everyone</strong>: What are some next steps? Everyone seems to agree on a smaller Slicer core; maybe we can spec this out together. Collaborate on the policy document Thibault created an initial copy of.</p>

<p>How do we process PRs coming in for now, until the longer-term solutions are worked out?</p>
<ul>
<li>Look over a certain number of PRs at each weekly hangout?</li>
